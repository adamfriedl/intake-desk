Title: Evidence and Reconsideration Basics (Educational)
Canonical references: agency self-help education

Benefits disputes often turn on missing verifications: income proof, household composition, immigration documents, medical paperwork, or residency evidence. Educational steps include reading exactly what the agency requested, submitting copies before deadlines, and keeping proof of submission.

Some programs allow reconsideration or conference options before or alongside a hearing. Use those pathways when available, but do not assume they pause appeal deadlines unless the rules say so.

Organize evidence with a cover list. Advocates can help decide what is persuasive versus irrelevant.
Evidence wins benefits appeals more often than eloquent cover letters alone. Match evidence to the stated denial reason. Income denials need pay stubs, bank statements, self-employment records, and household composition proof. Disability denials need medical records showing diagnosis, treatment, functional limitations, and consistency with SSA listings or Medicaid disability categories.

Organize submissions with a cover sheet listing documents enclosed and explaining relevance. Number pages if submitting large packets. Send copies unless originals are requested; keep duplicates.

SSA reconsideration allows new evidence. Submit treating physician statements describing work-related limitations. SSA-827 authorizes SSA to obtain records from providers—sign releases promptly.

Functional evidence for disability includes ADL questionnaires, mental health treatment notes, medication side effects, and third-party statements from family or former employers about observed limitations—not legal conclusions about disability status.

SNAP and Medicaid verifications: rent receipts proving shelter deduction, utility costs, child care expenses, and immigration status documents for eligible categories. Mixed-status households have complex rules—advocate review common.

Residency and identity: state ID, lease, utility bills in your name, or homeless service provider letters establish residency for state programs.

Reconsideration versus hearing: some agencies offer informal conferences before fair hearing. Use conferences to clarify missing documents but confirm whether conference attendance extends appeal deadlines—it usually does not.

Expert evidence: vocational experts appear at SSA ALJ hearings; clients rarely need their own expert at reconsideration but may bring lay witness testimony.

Timeliness: evidence submitted late before hearing may be excluded unless good cause shown. Submit early and supplement as new records arrive if rules allow.

Privacy: redact unrelated medical history when possible; include only records supporting claimed limitations.

Escalate when: denial based on records you never saw; agency failed to consider submitted evidence; need for expedited disability due to terminal illness (SSA compassionate allowances and TERI cases).

This digest does not obtain medical records on your behalf or predict disability findings.
Treat provider letters as supplements not substitutes for medical records—SSA and state agencies weigh objective clinical findings over conclusory "patient is disabled" statements without supporting findings.

School records for disabled children applying for SSI document IEP accommodations, disciplinary history related to disability, and attendance patterns.

Substance use disorders require evidence of treatment compliance and functional limits—denials often cite failure to follow prescribed treatment without exploring barriers.

Pain and fatigue conditions need frequency and duration documentation—diary templates from advocacy organizations help if treating providers lack detail.

Interpreter needs at hearings should be requested in writing before hearing date—failure to provide interpreter may be grounds for remand.

Electronic submission through agency portals creates timestamp proof—prefer portal upload when available over uncertified mail for evidence submissions.
Functional RFC forms from treating physician describing lift limits, sitting/standing hours, and absenteeism support disability cases more than diagnosis label alone.

Mental health evidence needs treatment frequency, medication trials, and functional decline—not just diagnosis of depression or PTSD.

Substance use in recovery: document treatment compliance and sobriety period—agency may deny if DAA material to disability finding.

Household wage stubs for SNAP must match employer letter—discrepancies cause denials; reconcile before submission.

Immigration documents for eligible non-citizen categories must be current—expired card may deny even if renewal pending; include renewal receipt.

School IEP and 504 plans for disabled child SSI applications document functional limits in school setting.

Alj hearing exhibits should be labeled and paginated—submit copy to SSA and retain identical copy for hearing.

Request SSA case file copy before hearing to see what evidence agency relied on in denial.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors.
