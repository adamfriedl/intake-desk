Title: How to Read a Determination Letter (Educational)
Canonical references: agency self-help education

Determination letters may approve, deny, reduce, or terminate benefits. Read for: household members covered, benefit amount, certification period, reporting requirements, and appeal language. Magical thinking about “they will fix it automatically” is risky—agencies often need a timely appeal or change report.

If multiple programs are mentioned (SNAP and Medicaid), track each decision separately. A phone call may clarify but should not replace preserving appeal rights in writing when deadlines loom.

Bring the full letter packet to intake, including inserts.
Determination letters approve, deny, reduce, or terminate benefits with different next steps for each. Approval letters specify benefit amount, certification period, reporting requirements, and renewal dates. Calendar renewal deadlines—many terminations are procedural failures to recertify, not true ineligibility.

Denial letters cite regulatory bases—compare to your facts. Reduction letters should show prior and new amounts with reason for change such as income increase or household size change.

Medicaid determination may reference MAGI household rules, tax filing relationships, and retroactive coverage election windows—typically three months prior in qualifying cases if applied timely.

SSA disability determinations follow a five-step sequential evaluation: substantial gainful activity, severity, meeting or equaling listings, past relevant work, and other work in economy. Denial letters often state which step failed—guides evidence strategy on appeal.

Concurrent letters: SSDI award with SSI offset, or Medicaid denial while SNAP approved, require separate tracking. One program's data may incorrectly flow to another—verify cross-program accuracy.

Authorized representative designations allow advocates or family to receive copies—Form SSA-1696 for SSA, comparable state forms for ODHS.

My Social Security online portal and ONE Oregon portal for state benefits provide decision histories—check for decisions never mailed due to address errors.

Quality control reviews may propose redeterminations months after approval—respond promptly.

Language access: request translated notices and interpreters for phone interviews if English is not your primary language—failure to provide access may be appealable.

Escalate when: denial contradicts prior approval without reported change; mathematical errors obvious; loss of medical coverage during active treatment; or inability to understand decision due to missing translations.

Bring full letter packets including envelopes and any referenced prior decision numbers to intake.
Online portals show decision status before mail arrives—check ONE Oregon and My Social Security regularly if expecting decisions.

Representative payee or authorized representative copies ensure someone else sees decisions if your mail is unreliable—homeless clients should use service provider addresses or authorized rep forms early.

Prior authorization denials for Medicaid-covered services differ from eligibility denials—appeal pathway may go through MCO internal process before fair hearing.

Cost-sharing and premium notices for OHP with income above free category require separate budgeting—termination for nonpayment of premium differs from income ineligibility.

Sequential SSA denials at initial and reconsideration stages look similar—read whether new evidence was considered at reconsideration before requesting ALJ hearing.

Translation quality matters for LEP clients—if translated determination contradicts English version or omits appeal rights, flag language access violation themes.
Cost sharing notices for OHP with premium require separate budgeting from SNAP food benefits—household may lose one while keeping other.

Medicare entitlement letters for SSDI recipients trigger Part B premium deductions—read Medicare insert with SSA award.

Prior authorization denial for prescription drug under Medicaid is pharmacy benefit dispute—appeal through CCO drug list exception process first.

Quality control (QC) sample denials after approval may demand repayment—treat QC notice as seriously as initial denial.

Authorized representative copies prevent missed deadlines when client homeless or incarcerated—establish rep early.

Online decision status "pending" for weeks may mean missing verification—proactive document submission faster than waiting for mailed denial.

Compare benefit calculation worksheet enclosed with determination—math errors in deduction or income counting common appeal grounds.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors. Oregon Law Help, consumer.ftc.gov, and usa.gov remain starting points for verifying that this educational summary matches current primary sources before acting on any single paragraph alone.

When a determination letter references a prior decision by number or date, locate that prior letter before the hearing. Hearing officers expect households to understand the chain of decisions. If the agency lost your change report, your submission proof—fax confirmations, portal screenshots, or office stamp receipts—becomes central evidence.
