Title: SNAP and Medicaid Denial Overview (Educational)
Canonical references: USDA SNAP public education; Medicaid.gov public education; Oregon ODHS public pages

SNAP (food benefits) and Medicaid (health coverage) have different eligibility rules, verification norms, and appeal systems, even when administered through related state agencies. Educational intake should identify which program denied or cut benefits and whether a medical emergency or food insecurity makes the matter urgent.

Common issues include income counting, household composition, immigration-related eligibility rules, and missed renewal paperwork. Renewals and recertifications generate many preventable terminations.

This overview orients retrieval; companion digests cover notice anatomy, appeals, evidence, and overpayments. Escalate urgent medical access problems for same-day advocate pathways when available.
SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) provides monthly food benefits on Oregon Trail cards. Eligibility turns on household size, gross and net income tests, resource limits for certain households, and work requirements for able-bodied adults without dependents in some cases. Oregon administers SNAP through ODHS with federal funding and rules.

Medicaid in Oregon primarily flows through Oregon Health Plan (OHP) with MAGI-based eligibility for many adults and children, plus separate categories for aged, blind, and disabled populations. Renewal periods and income reporting requirements differ from SNAP though ONE system may manage both.

Common SNAP denials: income over limit after overtime spike, failure to verify expenses, missed interview, failure to recertify, or voluntary quit from employment without good cause in work-registrant cases.

Common Medicaid denials: income above MAGI threshold, failure to verify citizenship or immigration status for eligible categories, missing renewal, or transfer of assets within look-back for certain long-term care categories not applicable to most MAGI cases.

Expedited SNAP serves households with little income and resources facing urgent food need—often within seven days if qualifying.

Retroactive Medicaid may cover three prior months of medical bills if eligible during those months and application timely.

Household composition rules: who buys and prepares food together for SNAP; who counts as tax household for Medicaid. Unrelated roommates may be separate SNAP households but Medicaid rules differ.

Student eligibility exceptions exist for SNAP in some circumstances—verify current student rules.

Immigration: qualified non-citizens may be eligible for some programs with waiting periods; others may enroll only specific members (e.g., citizen children). Mixed-status household rules are complex—escalate rather than guess.

Categorically eligible connections: receiving certain other benefits may simplify SNAP eligibility.

Documentation for intake: denial or termination notice, last approval notice, pay stubs, rent amount, utility costs, household member list with SSNs or immigration docs as required, and medical bills if seeking retroactive Medicaid.

ONE application renewal combines programs—partial completion may terminate one program while other continues, creating confusion. Confirm each program status separately after renewal submission.

Aged and disabled Medicaid pathways bypass MAGI rules with resource tests—older adults denied MAGI Medicaid may qualify under separate category if assets below limit.

Medicare Savings Programs help pay Medicare premiums for low-income seniors—distinct from OHP but related intake when elderly clients mention both.

SNAP work requirement waivers for homeless individuals and those with documented disabilities vary by policy year—verify current waiver status for ABAWD rules in Oregon.

Child support pass-through rules affect SNAP household income counting—bring court orders showing support amounts.

Emergency Medicaid for undocumented immigrants covers emergency conditions only—distinct from full OHP eligibility; hospital social workers often initiate these applications.
Oregon ONE system single application for SNAP and OHP—partial approval common; read each program decision paragraph.

Categorically eligible SNAP households bypass asset test in some cases—TANF or OHP receipt may simplify SNAP eligibility.

Medicaid MAGI for adults 19-64 differs from aged/blind/disabled Medicaid with resource test—elderly denied MAGI may qualify under other category.

SNAP work requirement ABAWD time limits in non-waiver areas—verify Oregon waiver status by county each federal fiscal year.

Student SNAP eligibility exceptions for college students working 20 hours or caring for child—document exception category.

Immigration: qualified immigrants five-year bar may apply; citizen children may get SNAP while parent ineligible—household composition rules allocate benefits.

Emergency Medicaid for labor and delivery covers delivery costs for qualifying emergencies regardless of immigration status in many cases—hospital billing office initiates.

OHP open enrollment continuous for eligible categories—unlike marketplace annual enrollment for private insurance.
