Title: SNAP and Medicaid Denial Overview (Educational)
Canonical references: USDA SNAP public education; Medicaid.gov public education; Oregon ODHS public pages

SNAP (food benefits) and Medicaid (health coverage) have different eligibility rules, verification norms, and appeal systems, even when administered through related state agencies. Educational intake should identify which program denied or cut benefits and whether a medical emergency or food insecurity makes the matter urgent.

Common issues include income counting, household composition, immigration-related eligibility rules, and missed renewal paperwork. Renewals and recertifications generate many preventable terminations.

This overview orients retrieval; companion digests cover notice anatomy, appeals, evidence, and overpayments. Escalate urgent medical access problems for same-day advocate pathways when available.
Additional educational context for advocates and lay readers:

Public benefits cases are paperwork-deadline cases. A well-explained story without a timely appeal can still lose the pathway to a hearing. Intake should capture the notice date, the stated deadline, the program name, and whether the household requested continuation of benefits pending appeal when that option exists.

Verification lists deserve a literal reading. Agencies often deny or close cases for missing documents even when eligibility might otherwise exist. Submitting partial packets early, with a cover letter listing what is enclosed and what is still coming, is a common educational practice—subject to program rules.

Intersecting issues are common: disability accommodations for notices and hearings, language access, domestic violence confidentiality, and homelessness can all affect how a household receives mail and complies with requests. Flag those facts for advocates rather than treating the case as a pure math eligibility problem.

This corpus does not compute benefit budgets or guarantee reinstatement. It supports grounded explanations of notice anatomy, appeal windows, evidence, overpayments, and program overview language for SNAP and Medicaid-style denials.
