Title: Unemployment Insurance in Oregon — Eligibility and Appeals
Canonical references: https://www.oregon.gov/employ/ ; Oregon Employment Department public materials

Oregon unemployment insurance (UI) provides temporary wage replacement when workers lose jobs through no fault of their own and meet work and earnings requirements. The Employment Department decides eligibility, benefit amount, and duration based on base period wages.

Common denial reasons: voluntary quit without good cause, misconduct discharge, refusal of suitable work, inability to work, fraud misrepresentation, and insufficient base period earnings.

Weekly claim certifications require reporting earnings, job contacts, and availability for work. Failure to certify stops payments even if initially approved.

Appeals of denial decisions must be filed within stated deadlines—often 20 days from mailing. Hearings are before administrative law judges; employer participates.

Overpayments occur if benefits paid during appeal later reversed or earnings underreported. Repayment and waiver rules apply—seek advocate help for large overpayments.

Partial employment reduces weekly benefit amount dollar-for-dollar after earnings disregard.

Documentation: determination letters, separation reason from employer, pay stubs, appeal request confirmations, and weekly claim history.

Escalate when: appeal deadline imminent; employer contests claim with false facts; overpayment threatens hardship; or benefits stopped mid-certification without explanation.

This digest does not certify weekly claims or predict benefit amounts.
Base period wage calculation uses first four of last five completed calendar quarters before claim—seasonal workers may have low quarters affecting benefit amount. Understand which wages counted before appealing denial for insufficient earnings.

Able and available requirements mean you must seek suitable work and accept offers unless good cause to refuse—document job search activities contemporaneously.

School employees and between-term claims face special eligibility rules—do not assume standard UI rules apply to education workers without checking Oregon Employment Department guidance.

Pandemic-era federal programs expired; current UI is state-funded with standard duration caps unless federal extensions enacted in future crises—verify current benefit weeks on official site.

Employer chargeability disputes rarely affect worker eligibility but may motivate employer to contest claim aggressively—bring separation letter and witness names to hearings.

Overpayment waivers require hardship and sometimes fault analysis—do not ignore overpayment letters while appealing underlying eligibility.
Oregon UI claimants must register with the iMatchSkills system and actively seek work unless waived for union hall attachment or approved training. Document job search contacts with employer name, date, method, and outcome. Failure to document search activities gives the Employment Department grounds to deny weeks even when initial claim approved.

Partial unemployment when hours reduced but not eliminated uses special reporting rules—report gross earnings each week before deductions. Misreporting triggers overpayment and potential fraud findings.

Union members with hiring hall rules may qualify for exceptions to work search—bring union documentation to appeals. Seasonal workers in agriculture and forestry should verify base period includes highest earning quarters.

Employer contests often allege misconduct discharge—bring employee handbook, termination letter, and witness statements to hearing. "Last straw" misconduct doctrine may apply when series of small incidents led to termination.

Appeal hearings are telephone or video in many regions—request accommodation if hearing impairment or language need exists. Continue certifying weekly claims during appeal when rules allow to preserve back benefits if you win.

If benefits exhausted, Oregon may offer extended benefits only when federal or state triggers activate—check current program status on official site rather than assuming extensions available.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors. If the Employment Department alleges you failed to accept suitable work, document why the offered job was unsuitable—wage far below prior earnings, unsafe conditions, or excessive commute may matter depending on facts and statutes. Keep screenshots of job offers and your written refusals or non-responses. Suitable work standards are contested frequently at hearings.

Escalate for human advocate review whenever safety, court dates, or program appeal windows are involved.
