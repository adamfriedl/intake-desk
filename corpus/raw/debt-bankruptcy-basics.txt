Title: Bankruptcy Basics for Consumer Debt — Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 Orientation
Canonical references: https://www.uscourts.gov/services-forms/bankruptcy ; https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/articles/debt-relief-or-bankruptcy

Bankruptcy is a federal court process offering relief from many unsecured debts through discharge (Chapter 7) or repayment plan (Chapter 13). Automatic stay stops most collection, garnishment, and foreclosure temporarily upon filing. Bankruptcy is not free—filing fees, credit counseling certificates, and attorney fees apply for most filers.

Chapter 7 liquidates non-exempt assets to pay creditors; Oregon exemption laws protect homestead, vehicle, household goods, and tools of trade up to limits. Most low-asset filers keep essential property. Means test may disqualify high-income filers from Chapter 7.

Chapter 13 proposes three- to five-year plans to pay secured arrears and partial unsecured debt; useful to save homes from foreclosure or cars from repossession when income is regular.

Debts commonly nondischargeable: recent taxes, child support, most student loans unless undue hardship proven, fraud debts, and some fines.

Credit impact: bankruptcy remains on credit reports for years but may improve long-term ability to rebuild versus endless defaults.

Credit counseling and debtor education courses are mandatory before and after filing.

Bankruptcy stops lawsuits but must be filed before judgment execution in many cases—earlier is often better.

Debt settlement companies cannot file bankruptcy; only attorneys or pro se filers petition the court.

Documentation for attorney consult: all debts listed, income six months, tax returns, lawsuits pending, transfers of property last years.

Escalate when: facing garnishment, foreclosure, or multiple judgments; consider bankruptcy consult through legal aid or bar referral.

This digest orients only—it does not recommend bankruptcy for your situation or prepare petitions.
Mandatory credit counseling must occur within 180 days before filing from an approved agency—certificate required with petition. Pre-filing planning includes gathering tax returns, pay stubs, bank statements, vehicle titles, and lawsuit lists for means test and schedules.

Chapter 13 may cure mortgage arrears over plan length while stopping foreclosure temporarily. Plan confirmation requires feasible income—irregular gig income complicates confirmation.

Co-signers remain liable on discharged debts in many cases—bankruptcy of primary borrower does not automatically protect co-signer from collection.

Student loans, recent luxury purchases, and cash advances shortly before filing may face scrutiny as nondischargeable or fraudulent—disclose all transactions honestly to counsel.

Post-bankruptcy credit rebuilding starts with secured cards and on-time housing payments; educational materials warn against predatory credit offers targeting recent filers.

Legal aid bankruptcy clinics accept limited cases by income; bar referral lists bankruptcy specialists for paid representation. Pro se filing is possible but risky for homeowners and wage earners with assets near exemption limits.
Credit counseling waiver available for disabled, incapacitated, active military in combat zone, and bankruptcy filers in areas without approved agencies—rare but verify if counseling inaccessible.

Homestead exemption in Oregon protects equity in primary residence up to statutory amount—verify current dollar limit before assuming home safe in Chapter 7.

Vehicle exemption allows one car up to equity limit—second vehicle may be non-exempt asset.

401k and most ERISA retirement accounts exempt—do not liquidate retirement to pay credit cards before bankruptcy consult.

Tax debt discharge rules: income tax may discharge if returns filed timely and debt old enough—three-year rule and other tests apply; trust fund payroll taxes not dischargeable.

Chapter 13 cramdown on car loan possible if loan old enough and car worth less than balance—specialized benefit of Chapter 13 over 7.

Co-debtor stay in Chapter 13 protects cosigner during plan—Chapter 7 lacks co-debtor stay.

Post-discharge creditor contact on discharged debts violates injunction—notify bankruptcy attorney with collector contact details.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors. Oregon Law Help, consumer.ftc.gov, and usa.gov remain starting points for verifying that this educational summary matches current primary sources before acting on any single paragraph alone.

Pre-bankruptcy transfers of assets to family members within years before filing can be clawed back by trustees. Do not sell or gift major assets to 'protect' them without bankruptcy counsel advice. Retirement accounts are usually protected, but cashing them out to pay credit cards destroys the exemption and creates tax consequences.
