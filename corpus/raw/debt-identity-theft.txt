Title: Identity Theft and Fraudulent Debt — Consumer Response Steps
Canonical references: https://www.identitytheft.gov/ ; https://consumer.ftc.gov/

Identity theft occurs when someone opens accounts or incurs debt using your personal information without authorization. Response steps: file a report at IdentityTheft.gov (FTC), obtain a recovery plan and Identity Theft Report, file a local police report if requested by creditors, and notify affected creditors and credit bureaus promptly.

Place fraud alerts or security freezes with credit bureaus. Freezes restrict new account openings; lifts are free when you apply for credit.

Dispute fraudulent tradelines with bureaus using FTC identity theft affidavit. Dispute same accounts with collectors in writing citing fraud.

Do not pay fraudulent debts to "make them go away" without clearing fraud status—payment may imply validity.

Collection lawsuits on fraudulent debts require answer raising fraud and lack of ownership; bring identity theft report to advocate immediately.

Tax-related identity theft uses IRS Form 14039—separate from consumer credit fraud.

Mixed files: your legitimate accounts alongside fraudulent ones need itemized disputes per account.

Documentation: FTC report, police report, bureau dispute confirmations, collector letters, proof you did not open account (if available).

Escalate when: sued; arrested warrant related to identity confusion; repeated new fraudulent accounts after freeze; or domestic abuser opened credit in your name.

This digest does not investigate criminals or guarantee credit deletion timelines.
Child identity theft requires parent/guardian disputes with bureaus using FTC minor identity theft procedures. Schools and medical offices are common leak points—ask who accessed child's SSN.

Employment identity theft mixes IRS wage reporting with credit fraud—SSA earnings statements may show wages from employers you never worked for; correct through SSA and IRS identity theft units.

Domestic abusers and family members sometimes open accounts in victim names—document relationship and whether you had access to mail when accounts opened.

Debt collectors may resist fraud findings until police report and FTC report attached—persist with written disputes citing FCRA fraud block rights.

If criminal charges arise from identity confusion (checks cashed in your name), route criminal overlap immediately while civil fraud disputes continue.

Keep a log of every bureau, creditor, and collector contact with confirmation numbers—identity theft recovery spans months and requires organizational discipline.
Extended fraud alert and credit freeze should remain until recovery complete—document each lift when applying for legitimate credit.

Affidavit of fraud to collectors must be specific: account number, why fraudulent, police report number, and statement you did not open account or authorize user.

Employer HR departments may help when wages reported from fake employment—SSA mismatch letter procedure.

Child identity theft recovery may require contacting bureaus with parent ID and birth certificate—plan longer timeline than adult fraud.

If collector sues on fraudulent account, raise fraud defense in answer and attach FTC identity theft report—default judgment on fraud debt creates bigger cleanup later.

Domestic violence financial abuse documentation may support expedited bureau blocks and fraud disputes—coordinate with domestic violence advocate for safety planning while disputing accounts abuser opened.

Keep recovery log for years—fraudulent accounts sometimes reappear when sold to new debt buyer who did not receive prior fraud notation.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors. Oregon Law Help, consumer.ftc.gov, and usa.gov remain starting points for verifying that this educational summary matches current primary sources before acting on any single paragraph alone.

Place a free security freeze at all three bureaus before closing identity theft intake if new accounts keep appearing. Unfreeze only temporarily when you must apply for legitimate credit. Provide advocates truncated account numbers rather than full numbers when possible, and store full numbers only in secure personal files rather than email threads.

Escalate for human advocate review whenever safety, court dates, or program appeal windows are involved.
