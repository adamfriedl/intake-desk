Title: Wage Garnishment Overview (Educational)
Canonical references: https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/ ; federal consumer education on garnishments

Wage garnishment generally requires a legal process—often a court judgment—before a portion of pay can be withheld, with important exceptions for certain government debts (for example some federal student loans or tax debts) that follow different rules. Educational materials warn consumers that collectors may threaten garnishment before they have legal authority.

Exemptions and caps may limit how much can be taken from wages or bank accounts depending on the debt type and jurisdiction. Oregon-specific exemption details should be confirmed with primary sources or an advocate.

If a garnishment starts, act quickly: read employer notices, court papers, and exemption claim instructions. This digest does not calculate exemption amounts.
Wage garnishment for ordinary consumer debt typically requires a court judgment. The collector must sue, win (or obtain default), and follow state procedures to garnish wages. Collectors who threaten immediate garnishment without a judgment often bluff—document the threat and consider complaints. Exceptions include some federal debts (tax, federal student loans) with administrative garnishment paths without a private lawsuit.

Federal law caps garnishment of disposable earnings at 25% or the amount exceeding 30 times the federal minimum wage per week, whichever is less, for many consumer judgments. Oregon exemption law may protect additional amounts or categories of income. Social Security and some benefits receive special protection from bank levies though commingling in accounts complicates protection.

Bank account levies freeze funds after judgment. Exemption claim forms may allow you to protect certain deposits if filed timely—deadlines are short, sometimes as little as ten days from notice. Seek same-week help when a levy hits.

Garnishment notices go to your employer, who must withhold and remit unless an exemption applies. Employer retaliation for one garnishment is generally prohibited under federal law, though multiple garnishments create HR complications.

Before garnishment, a lawsuit usually occurred. If you never received suit papers, default judgment may be improper due to bad service—consult an advocate about setting aside default in your state court.

Oregon-specific exemption amounts and procedures should be verified on Oregon Law Help and current ORS exemption statutes. Homestead, vehicle, and tools-of-trade exemptions may matter for bank levies beyond wages.

Student loan and tax garnishment follow different notice and hearing rights. Federal student loan administrative wage garnishment allows a hearing request in some circumstances before garnishment begins.

If garnishment would leave you unable to afford basic living expenses, bankruptcy or hardship programs may be options advocates discuss—see bankruptcy basics digest for orientation only.

Documentation for intake: judgment papers, writ of garnishment, employer notice, pay stubs showing disposable income, bank levy notice, and exemption claim forms filed or available.

Escalate immediately upon: first levy or garnishment notice, garnishment of clearly exempt benefits, or garnishment without remembered lawsuit. Medium urgency: collector threats of imminent garnishment—verify court records online if available in your county.

This digest does not calculate exempt amounts or file exemption claims for you.
