Title: Disputing Errors on Credit Reports (FTC Public Education)

Your credit report affects whether you can get a loan, a job, insurance, or an apartment. It is important that the information in your report is complete and accurate.

You are entitled to a free copy of your credit report every 12 months from each of the three nationwide credit reporting companies. You can request reports through the official centralized service. Review reports from all three companies because they may contain different information.

If you find mistakes — wrong accounts, incorrect balances, accounts that are not yours, or outdated negative information — you can dispute them with the credit reporting company and with the company that furnished the information.

To dispute an error, write to the credit reporting company and explain what is wrong. Include copies (not originals) of documents that support your position. Identify each item you dispute, state the facts, and ask that the error be corrected. Send your letter by certified mail and keep a copy.

The credit reporting company must investigate most disputes within 30 days unless it considers your dispute frivolous. It must forward your dispute to the furnisher, which must investigate and report back. When the investigation is complete, the credit reporting company must give you the results in writing and a free copy of your report if the dispute changes the report.

If the furnisher continues to report incorrect information, you may need to dispute again or seek advocate help. Negative information generally may stay on a credit report for a limited number of years depending on the type of entry, but inaccurate information should be corrected or removed.

Checking your own report is a "soft inquiry" and does not lower your score. Disputing errors is separate from responding to a debt collection lawsuit — court deadlines still apply if you are sued.

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) governs credit reporting companies (CRAs) and entities that furnish information to them. You have the right to dispute incomplete or inaccurate information. CRAs must investigate most disputes within 30 days (sometimes 45 if you provide additional information during investigation). Furnishers must investigate and correct inaccurate reports.

Obtain reports from all three major bureaus—Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion—because furnishers may report to only one or two. AnnualCreditReport.com is the authorized free source; beware imposter sites. During certain periods, additional free weekly reports may be available under federal initiatives—verify current policies at consumer.ftc.gov.

Dispute packages should include: your identifying information, each disputed tradeline by account number, specific factual errors (wrong balance, not my account, paid in full, wrong dates), and supporting documents such as payment confirmations, court orders, or identity theft reports. Send to the CRA and consider copying the furnisher.

Results of investigation must be provided in writing. If corrected, request an updated free report. If unchanged, you may add a brief consumer statement explaining your side, though statements rarely change scores.

Negative but accurate information may remain for legally permitted periods—commonly seven years for many delinquencies, with variations for bankruptcies and certain public records. Accurate negative information is not removable simply because it hurts your score. Disputes target errors, not discomfort with truthful reporting.

Medical debt reporting rules have evolved; CFPB and credit industry policies may limit certain medical collections on reports. Verify current rules rather than assuming all medical debt is excluded. Dispute inaccurate medical tradelines regardless of category.

Hard inquiries from credit applications differ from soft inquiries from your own review. Disputing your own legitimate inquiries rarely succeeds.

If a furnisher verifies inaccurate information, next steps may include CFPB complaint, follow-up dispute with additional documents, or advocate consultation about furnisher liability under FCRA.

Credit reports affect housing applications, employment background checks in permitted jurisdictions, and insurance in some states. Fixing errors before applying for housing may matter as much as score improvement.

Escalate when: identity theft accounts appear; same debt reports multiple times inflating damage; debts discharged in bankruptcy still show as owed; or CRA fails to respond within statutory time.
