Title: Debt Collection Communications — What Collectors Can and Cannot Do (FTC Public Education)

Debt collectors contact people who owe money to creditors. Sometimes the collector works for the original creditor; sometimes it is a separate debt buyer. The rules depend in part on whether the collector is covered by the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, which generally applies to third-party collectors collecting debts on behalf of others.

Collectors may contact you by phone, mail, email, or text. They must identify themselves as debt collectors in every communication. They must tell you that any information they obtain will be used to collect the debt.

Collectors may not contact you at inconvenient times or places. They generally may not call before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local time unless you agree. They may not contact you at work if you tell them your employer prohibits such calls.

Harassment is illegal. Collectors may not use threats of violence, obscene language, or repeated calls intended to annoy. They may not publish lists of people who refuse to pay debts. They may not misrepresent the character, amount, or legal status of a debt.

Collectors may not lie. They may not falsely claim to be attorneys or government representatives. They may not threaten arrest for ordinary consumer debt. They may not tell you legal papers are not legal forms when they are, or vice versa.

You can ask a collector to stop contacting you. If you send a written request to stop contact, the collector may only contact you to confirm there will be no further contact or to notify you of a specific action such as filing a lawsuit. Keep a copy of your letter.

A collection call does not by itself mean you have been sued. A lawsuit usually involves formal service of court papers with response deadlines. Treat summonses separately from collection letters.

If you believe a collector broke the law, document each contact and consider reporting to the FTC, CFPB, and your state attorney general. An advocate can help evaluate whether you have additional remedies.

This excerpt supports intake about collection calls and letters. It does not recommend paying, settling, or ignoring any specific account.
