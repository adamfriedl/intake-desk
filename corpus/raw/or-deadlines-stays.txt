Title: Deadlines and Temporary Stays (Educational)
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/ ; Oregon court self-help education

Housing cases are deadline-driven. Notice periods, first-appearance dates, trial settings, and post-judgment enforcement windows can be short. Educational materials stress calendaring every date on the summons and any later court orders.

A “stay” generally means a temporary pause of enforcement. Whether a stay is available depends on the case posture, local rules, and sometimes payment or appeal conditions. There is no universal guarantee of more time. Asking the court or negotiating with the landlord’s counsel are fact-specific paths that advocates often evaluate.

Do not rely on informal promises that “the sheriff won’t come” without written confirmation and advocate advice. Utility shutoff threats, lockout threats, and court dates can overlap—track each separately.

This digest flags deadline risk. It does not list a single statewide emergency stay form or promise a continuance.
Housing disputes run on multiple overlapping clocks. Notice periods govern when a tenancy may end. Court answer deadlines govern whether a default judgment enters. Trial settings, mediation dates, writ issuance, and sheriff scheduling add more layers. A tenant focused only on the notice expiration may miss a court date that occurs earlier or later than expected. Build one master calendar with every date from every document.

Notice deadlines are usually measured from delivery or posting according to statute and lease. Court deadlines are usually measured from service of the summons. These are different events. A 72-hour nonpayment notice does not tell you the FED first appearance date—you must read the summons for that.

Stays of execution temporarily pause the sheriff from enforcing a writ of possession. Availability depends on local court rules, whether rent is paid into court, whether an appeal is pending, and sometimes seasonal or hardship policies. There is no statewide guarantee of a stay because you asked for more time. Some courts require a formal motion; others handle stays informally at the first appearance.

Continuances reschedule hearings but do not always stop enforcement if a judgment already entered. Tenants sometimes confuse a friendly landlord promise to "hold off the sheriff" with a legal stay. Without a court order or written filed agreement, enforcement can proceed.

Rent tender and cure periods have their own deadlines. Paying rent after a nonpayment notice may reinstate the tenancy if done correctly and within the notice window, but partial payment or late payment may not. Document every payment method and recipient.

Appeals from FED judgments exist in some circumstances but are specialized and time-limited. Self-represented tenants often need same-week advocate assistance after an adverse judgment if they explore appeal.

Overlapping emergencies complicate deadline tracking: a tenant facing writ enforcement who also receives a utility shutoff notice must prioritize safety, court, and housing stability simultaneously. Flag compound urgency for human triage.

Practical tools: photograph every court paper and highlight dates in color; set phone reminders three days before each deadline; send court filings by a method that produces proof; keep copies of everything filed. If you cannot meet a deadline, contact legal aid before the date passes—not after.

When to escalate: any deadline within 72 hours, sheriff notice posted on your door, or conflicting dates on notice versus summons. This digest does not promise a continuance or stay will be granted.

Electronic filing timestamps control if court requires e-file—paper filers should use courthouse drop box before midnight deadlines where local rule allows.

Payment into court registry to stay execution may require exact amount and proper caption—clerk rejection for technical defect wastes critical days.

Sheriff writ posting on door starts final countdown—photograph writ and call advocate same day if not already represented.

Landlord promise to cancel case if rent paid by Friday requires filed dismissal or written stipulation—verify case status on court docket online after payment.
