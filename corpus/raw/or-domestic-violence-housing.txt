Title: Domestic Violence, Sexual Assault, and Stalking — Housing Protections in Oregon
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/ ; VAWA HUD materials for subsidized housing

Survivors of domestic violence, sexual assault, and stalking face housing instability from abuser conduct, landlord reactions to police calls, and lease violations caused by abusers. Oregon law and federal VAWA provide protections that standard eviction advice may miss.

Early lease termination without penalty may be available when survivors certify abuse and meet statutory notice requirements to landlord. Documentation may include police reports, protective orders, or qualified third-party statements depending on statute—verify current forms on Oregon Law Help.

Lock changes and security measures may be requested; landlords may have duties to accommodate reasonable safety requests in some contexts. Subsidized housing VAWA protections allow bifurcating leases to remove abusers while survivors remain, and prohibit termination based on criminal activity of abuser in qualifying cases.

Eviction for noise or damage caused by abuser may be defensible with VAWA certification or state law defenses. Calls to police for domestic violence are not nuisance violations in many protected frameworks.

Relocation assistance and emergency shelter referrals are safety priorities—legal aid domestic violence units coordinate with advocates.

Children and school stability: McKinney-Vento education rights may apply if housing loss occurs—flag for family advocates.

Documentation: protective orders, police report numbers, photos of injuries or property damage, messages threatening harm, and landlord notices timed after abuse reports.

Escalate all same-day safety emergencies to domestic violence hotlines first, then housing legal aid. Never require survivors to confront abusers for housing paperwork.

This digest supports intake triage; it does not file protective orders or guarantee lease termination approval.
Safety planning precedes legal strategy. National and local domestic violence hotlines help with confidential shelter, safety plans, and referrals before lease termination paperwork. Legal aid domestic violence units often require screening separate from general housing intake—disclose abuse early so routing is correct.

Protective orders may include stay-away provisions affecting shared housing. Violations of protective orders are criminal matters—route criminal overlap per referral digest while civil housing advocates handle lease bifurcation.

Children's school records and custody orders affect where survivors can safely relocate. Educational stability laws may help when housing changes force school moves mid-year.

Financial abuse intersects with debt corpus topics: abusers may control bank accounts, ruin credit, or intercept mail containing benefits notices. Flag financial control when intake mentions both abuse and missed appeal deadlines.

VAWA certification forms for subsidized housing have specific content requirements and deadlines. Missing VAWA deadlines can waive protections even when abuse is documented elsewhere—high urgency for subsidized tenants.
Address confidentiality programs (Safe at Home) may provide substitute mailing address for survivors—ask advocate about enrollment before filing housing applications that require address disclosure to abuser.

Lease bifurcation removes abuser from lease while survivor stays—housing authority and private landlords have different procedures under VAWA and state law.

Emergency lock change laws may allow survivor to change locks at own expense with notice to landlord in qualifying situations—verify statute before changing locks.

Children's records in custody disputes affect where survivor can safely live—family law overlap requires coordinated referrals.

Stalking through technology (cameras, smart locks controlled by abuser) creates habitability and safety issues in rental—document device access and request landlord reset credentials.

Immigration-sensitive survivors may fear reporting abuse—VAWA self-petition and U visa pathways are immigration specialist matters; civil housing protections may still apply without immigration resolution.

Employer EAP and domestic violence leave laws may provide income during relocation—employment law referral separate from housing defense.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors. Oregon Law Help, consumer.ftc.gov, and usa.gov remain starting points for verifying that this educational summary matches current primary sources before acting on any single paragraph alone.

Survivors should not be asked to choose between safety and housing stability without advocate support. Many Oregon communities coordinate confidential shelter, legal aid, and voucher specialists through domestic violence service networks. If police responded to the residence, obtain report numbers when safe to do so; reports can support housing certification forms but are not always required.
