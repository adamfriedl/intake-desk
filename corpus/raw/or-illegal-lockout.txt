Title: Illegal Lockouts and Self-Help Eviction (Oregon Educational)
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/

A landlord generally may not lock a tenant out, shut off essential utilities, or seize belongings to force someone out without going through the required court process. These self-help tactics are often described as illegal lockouts or unlawful ouster in educational materials.

If a lockout happens, safety comes first. Document what occurred: photos of changed locks, utility shutoffs, messages from the landlord, and an inventory of removed property if known. Contact local legal aid, a tenant hotline, or an emergency advocate pathway where available. In some situations law enforcement or code agencies may take reports, but civil housing remedies are usually the core path.

Do not assume you must immediately sign a new agreement under pressure. Avoid violence or property damage. If an FED case is also pending, calendar court dates separately from the lockout facts—both may matter.

This digest is decision-support only. Immediate lockout situations are high urgency and should be flagged for human review.
Additional educational context for advocates and lay readers:

Jurisdiction and local overlays matter. Statewide landlord-tenant concepts may be modified by city ordinances addressing just-cause termination, relocation assistance, or screening criteria. Always compare the lease, the notice, and any local rules that apply to the property address.

Communication hygiene reduces later disputes. Prefer dated written messages for repair requests, payment questions, and occupancy changes. If conversations happen by phone or in person, follow up with a short written summary. Preserve envelopes and proof of mailing when notices arrive by post.

Safety and urgency triage should sit alongside legal triage. Illegal lockouts, utility shutoffs, and imminent first appearances are high urgency. Habitability problems that threaten health—no heat in cold weather, sewage backups, or gas leaks—also warrant rapid escalation even before every document is perfectly organized.
