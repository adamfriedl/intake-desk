Title: Illegal Lockouts and Self-Help Eviction (Oregon Educational)
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/

A landlord generally may not lock a tenant out, shut off essential utilities, or seize belongings to force someone out without going through the required court process. These self-help tactics are often described as illegal lockouts or unlawful ouster in educational materials.

If a lockout happens, safety comes first. Document what occurred: photos of changed locks, utility shutoffs, messages from the landlord, and an inventory of removed property if known. Contact local legal aid, a tenant hotline, or an emergency advocate pathway where available. In some situations law enforcement or code agencies may take reports, but civil housing remedies are usually the core path.

Do not assume you must immediately sign a new agreement under pressure. Avoid violence or property damage. If an FED case is also pending, calendar court dates separately from the lockout facts—both may matter.

This digest is decision-support only. Immediate lockout situations are high urgency and should be flagged for human review.
An illegal lockout occurs when a landlord or agent excludes a tenant from the rental unit without a court order authorizing possession. Changing locks, removing doors, shutting off essential utilities to force departure, removing belongings, or threatening violence to make someone leave are classic self-help eviction tactics prohibited in educational summaries of Oregon landlord-tenant law. Even if rent is unpaid or the lease has expired, the landlord generally must follow notice and court process before physical removal.

Utility shutoffs pose special urgency. Heat, water, and electricity affect health and safety. If a landlord controls master utilities and shuts them off to pressure move-out, document meter readings, billing records, and any written or recorded statements linking the shutoff to the dispute. Tenants may need to contact utility companies, code enforcement, and legal aid simultaneously. Restoring service can take days; temporary shelter planning may be necessary.

If you are locked out, prioritize personal safety and immediate access to medication, children, and identification documents. Call legal aid or a tenant hotline the same day if possible. Some areas have emergency court procedures for lockout relief, but availability varies by county. Avoid breaking back in if that could be characterized as criminal trespass or property damage—advocates can advise on lawful re-entry paths depending on facts.

Document the lockout thoroughly: timestamped photos of changed locks, utility shutoff notices, text messages from the landlord, witness names, and police report numbers if you filed a report. Police sometimes treat lockouts as civil matters, but a report still creates a record. Keep any "new agreement" or "cash for keys" paper pushed on you during a lockout; signing under duress is an advocate issue.

Lockouts sometimes occur alongside pending or threatened FED cases. A court case does not automatically authorize the landlord to lock you out before judgment and writ enforcement. Conversely, a lockout does not eliminate the need to respond to court papers if you were served. Track both threads on separate calendar entries.

Common scenarios: landlord says you abandoned the unit and removed your property—disputes over abandonment require evidence of continued occupancy such as mail receipt, utilities in your name, and personal belongings inside. Roommate lockouts by a master tenant raise different legal relationships; see the roommate digest. Subsidized housing lockouts still require process; voucher termination is separate from physical removal.

When to escalate: any same-day lockout, utility shutoff for nonpayment of rent without lawful notice, removal of belongings, or threats of force. When police are involved and someone is at risk of violence, safety planning comes before document gathering. Medium urgency: landlord posted a notice on the door saying you must leave tonight without court papers.

Jurisdictional note: mobile home park evictions and RV park tenancies may follow related but distinct rules. This digest focuses on conventional residential rentals under ORLA themes; park-specific materials apply to space rentals.
Temporary restraining order for lockout may be available in extreme cases—courthouse domestic violence or civil clinic may assist same day in some counties.

Hotel costs during illegal lockout may be recoverable in civil claim themes—keep itemized receipts if advocate pursues damages.

Storage of removed belongings in warehouse with daily fees—landlord improper storage may limit tenant liability for storage charges.

Police standoff when landlord and tenant both present at lockout—remain calm; ask police to document civil nature and refer to court.

Re-keying by tenant after illegal lockout risky without court order—advocate should advise lawful re-entry method for your county.

Landlord admission in text message that they changed locks "because you didn't pay" documents self-help eviction intent—preserve messages.
