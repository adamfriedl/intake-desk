Title: Mobile and Manufactured Home Park Tenancies in Oregon
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/ ; ORS chapter 90 mobile home park provisions

Mobile home park residents often own the home but rent the pad (space). Oregon law treats many space rentals under ORLA with additional park-specific protections. Eviction from the pad can mean losing your home if you cannot afford relocation—stakes exceed typical apartment tenancies.

Park rules, rent increases, and termination notices must follow statutory notice lengths and content for space rentals. Community-wide rule changes require proper notice. Arbitrary rule enforcement may support retaliation or discrimination claims.

Buyer protection when purchasing homes in parks: review park rules, rent history, and park closure risks before buying. Park closures trigger relocation assistance themes in some statutes—verify current law.

Habitability for pad rental includes utility infrastructure, road maintenance, common areas, and services promised in rental agreement. Home interior repairs may be owner responsibility unless lease assigns otherwise.

Rent-to-own and seller-financed home sales in parks create consumer issues beyond landlord-tenant law—escalate hybrid cases.

VAWA and fair housing apply in many parks receiving federal assistance or meeting coverage tests.

Documentation: space rental agreement, park rules, home title or purchase papers, rent and fee receipts, termination notices, and photos of park conditions.

Escalate when: park attempts pad termination without notice; rent increase on space violates caps; park blocks home sale to approved buyer; or closure notice received.

This digest does not value mobile homes or resolve title disputes between home sellers and buyers.
Financing and home ownership create repossession risks separate from pad eviction. If you financed the home through the seller or a lender, default on home loan may lead to home repossession even while pad rent is current. Identify whether intake concerns pad rental, home loan, or both.

Park closures and conversions to other land uses trigger relocation themes under Oregon educational summaries. Tenants facing park closure should preserve closure notices, meeting minutes, and relocation assistance offers in writing.

Utility billing in parks—whether water, sewer, and garbage are included in space rent or billed separately—affects habitability and rent increase analysis. Review park rules and prior billing history.

Guest and occupancy rules in parks may restrict family members living in the home. Fair housing familial status protections may conflict with overly restrictive guest policies—escalate discrimination themes.

Selling a mobile home in place requires park approval of buyer in many parks. Unreasonable refusal of qualified buyers may be challengeable under park transfer rules—document buyer applications and park responses.
Manufactured home title with DMV differs from vehicle title—liens on title affect sale and repossession separately from pad rental.

Park rules about skirting, roofs, and add-ons may affect habitability and resale—violations may lead to fees or termination if uncured.

Water and septic in rural parks create environmental habitability issues—test results and health department orders support repair demands.

Rent control exemptions for newer parks or spaces may differ from apartment rent cap rules—verify park-specific statutes.

Tenant-owned home with landlord-owned pad means two potential court cases—pad FED and home repossession—coordinate both with advocate.

Oregon Manufactured Housing Commission and landlord-tenant advocates publish park-specific educational materials beyond standard apartment ORLA summaries.

When park changes ownership, successor must honor existing space rental terms until proper notice of change—document ownership transition notices.
Practical documentation recap: keep originals safe, provide copies to advocates, and maintain a dated timeline of contacts with landlords, collectors, or agencies. When deadlines approach within one week, treat the matter as urgent intake regardless of other life stressors. Oregon Law Help, consumer.ftc.gov, and usa.gov remain starting points for verifying that this educational summary matches current primary sources before acting on any single paragraph alone.

Pad rent increases in parks follow space-rental notice rules that may differ from apartment notice lengths. Compare the increase notice to park rules incorporated in your rental agreement and to any park-wide rule change bulletin. Document whether the increase applies to all spaces equally or only your lot, because selective increases may support discrimination or retaliation review.

Escalate for human advocate review whenever safety, court dates, or program appeal windows are involved.
