Title: Roommates and Subtenants (Oregon Educational)
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/

Roommate disputes are often messier than classic landlord-tenant cases because rights depend on who is on the lease. A person on the lease usually has a direct relationship with the landlord. A roommate who is not on the lease may be a guest, subtenant, or occupant with fewer or different protections, depending on facts and agreements.

If one roommate wants another to leave, changing locks or threatening removal can create legal and safety problems—especially if the person has established residency. Written roommate agreements help but do not override the landlord’s lease or local law.

When the landlord is trying to evict, every adult occupant may be affected even if only one signed. Subsidized housing and school housing may add extra rules.

Gather: lease, roommate agreement, payment proofs, messages about occupancy, and any notices. Escalate for advocate review when lockouts, violence, or eviction summonses are involved.
Roommate situations split into legal categories that determine who can evict whom and who owes rent to the landlord. Co-tenants are all on the lease and generally have equal rights and joint liability—the landlord can hold any co-tenant responsible for the full rent. A master tenant on the lease who sublets to a roommate may have landlord-like duties to the subtenant depending on facts. A person never on the lease may still be a tenant at sufferance or have established tenancy rights through length of occupancy and payment patterns.

Joint and several liability means the landlord can pursue one co-tenant for the entire unpaid rent even if that person paid their "share." Internal agreements splitting rent among roommates do not bind the landlord. If one roommate moves out, the remaining tenants may still owe full rent unless the landlord agrees to a lease modification.

Unauthorized occupants can trigger for-cause notices against the leaseholder. Adding a roommate without permission violates many leases. Conversely, a long-term occupant not on the lease may acquire protections that make informal eviction by the master tenant illegal. Locking out a roommate without court process can expose the lockout actor to civil liability.

When the landlord files FED against all occupants, each named defendant should respond. Being unnamed on the lease does not always mean you can ignore court papers if you are listed as a defendant. Subsidized housing rules about household composition can terminate vouchers if unreported members lived in the unit.

Domestic violence between roommates raises safety and housing law intersections. The survivor may need protection orders, lock changes, or lease bifurcation options. Treat violence as a safety emergency first.

Practical documentation: the lease showing all signers, any roommate agreement, payment records showing who paid the landlord, messages about move-in/move-out dates, and notices naming specific individuals. For disputes among roommates only (no landlord involvement), small claims for money owed may be separate from eviction defense.

Escalate when: a roommate locked you out, the landlord threatens eviction based on one roommate's conduct, subsidized housing claims unapproved household members, or you receive FED papers while not on the lease. This digest does not determine whether you are a tenant or guest—that is fact-specific under Oregon law.
Cash rent paid to master tenant without receipts creates proof problems when master tenant disputes your tenancy—use traceable payment methods when possible.

Sexual harassment by master tenant or landlord toward roommate may trigger fair housing and VAWA themes distinct from ordinary roommate money disputes.

College housing and university dormitories fall outside this digest's ORLA focus—route to campus housing office and student legal services.

Airbnb and short-term rental occupants generally lack ORLA tenancy protections—duration and agreement type matter for whether ORLA applies.

Assignment and sublease clauses in lease may prohibit subletting entirely—master tenant subletting in violation of lease exposes everyone to landlord for-cause termination.

Utility account names establish occupancy evidence—putting utilities in your name may help prove residency in roommate disputes.
All tenants on lease jointly liable for full rent—internal split agreement does not reduce landlord claim against any co-tenant.

Master tenant subletting without landlord permission may violate lease and trigger everyone’s termination—get landlord written consent for subtenants.

Guest versus tenant distinction uses length of stay and rent payment—long-term guest may acquire tenancy rights.

Sexual harassment between roommates in same rental may implicate landlord duty depending on control and notice—escalate complex facts.

College roommate departure mid-lease: remaining tenants owe full rent unless landlord releases departing tenant in writing.

Security deposit return to last remaining co-tenant may not reflect internal splits—roommate civil dispute separate from landlord deposit accounting.

Unauthorized lockout by master tenant—same illegal lockout themes; police may treat as civil; legal aid for re-entry advice.

Subsidized housing unreported roommate violates program rules—report changes to housing authority before guest becomes permanent.

Written roommate agreement helps internal disputes but cannot evict without court process—no self-help lockout among roommates.
