Title: Security Deposits and Move-Out (Oregon Educational)
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/

Security deposits are money held to cover unpaid rent or damage beyond ordinary wear and tear. Educational materials typically emphasize written move-in and move-out condition documentation, photos, and a clear forwarding address for deposit accounting.

After a tenancy ends, landlords often must provide an accounting of deductions within a statutory timeline and return any remaining balance. Deductible items commonly argued about include unpaid rent, cleaning beyond ordinary wear, and damage. Ordinary wear and tear is not the same as damage caused by abuse or negligence, though disputes are fact-specific.

If the deposit is not returned or the accounting seems improper, tenants may have civil claim options under residential landlord-tenant rules. Deadlines and damage multipliers, if any, depend on statute and facts. Keep the lease, payment proofs, condition photos, and all deposit correspondence.

This digest does not calculate a specific dollar recovery. For disputed deductions, gather documents and seek advocate review before filing anything.
Additional educational context for advocates and lay readers:

Jurisdiction and local overlays matter. Statewide landlord-tenant concepts may be modified by city ordinances addressing just-cause termination, relocation assistance, or screening criteria. Always compare the lease, the notice, and any local rules that apply to the property address.

Communication hygiene reduces later disputes. Prefer dated written messages for repair requests, payment questions, and occupancy changes. If conversations happen by phone or in person, follow up with a short written summary. Preserve envelopes and proof of mailing when notices arrive by post.

Safety and urgency triage should sit alongside legal triage. Illegal lockouts, utility shutoffs, and imminent first appearances are high urgency. Habitability problems that threaten health—no heat in cold weather, sewage backups, or gas leaks—also warrant rapid escalation even before every document is perfectly organized.
