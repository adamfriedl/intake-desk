Title: Oregon Tenant Rights Overview
Canonical references: https://oregonlawhelp.org/ ; Oregon Residential Landlord and Tenant Act concepts (ORS chapter 90 education)

Oregon renters generally have rights related to habitability, proper notice before termination, protection from illegal lockouts, and rules about security deposits. The exact notice period and remedies depend on the tenancy type (fixed-term lease vs month-to-month), the reason for ending the tenancy, and local ordinances that may add protections in cities such as Portland.

A landlord usually cannot end a tenancy without following required notice procedures. Self-help evictions—changing locks, shutting off utilities, or removing belongings without a court process—are not a lawful way to remove a tenant. When a landlord seeks possession through court, the case is commonly called a Forcible Entry and Detainer (FED) action.

Tenants should document communications, keep copies of the lease and notices, photograph conditions, and calendar deadlines. Missing a response deadline in an eviction case can lead to a default judgment. This overview is intentionally high-level: more specific digests in this corpus cover notice types, habitability, deposits, court process, and documentation checklists.

If immigration status, domestic violence, disability accommodations, or subsidized housing vouchers are involved, escalate for specialized advocate review because additional federal or program rules may apply.
Additional educational context for advocates and lay readers:

Jurisdiction and local overlays matter. Statewide landlord-tenant concepts may be modified by city ordinances addressing just-cause termination, relocation assistance, or screening criteria. Always compare the lease, the notice, and any local rules that apply to the property address.

Communication hygiene reduces later disputes. Prefer dated written messages for repair requests, payment questions, and occupancy changes. If conversations happen by phone or in person, follow up with a short written summary. Preserve envelopes and proof of mailing when notices arrive by post.

Safety and urgency triage should sit alongside legal triage. Illegal lockouts, utility shutoffs, and imminent first appearances are high urgency. Habitability problems that threaten health—no heat in cold weather, sewage backups, or gas leaks—also warrant rapid escalation even before every document is perfectly organized.
